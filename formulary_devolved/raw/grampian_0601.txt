 06.01.01

 Insulins 
   [img: note]   
 Insulin initiation should be delivered by an appropriately trained health care professional as part of a structure programme that includes active insulin dose titration, injection technique, self monitoring of blood glucose, hypoglycaemia education, driving advice, dietary awareness and ongoing support with appropriate health care professionals.
 People should not be switched between brands without consultation and agreement. 
People already established on insulin should not be switched without their agreement and can continue on a preparation that may not be routinely recommended in NHS Grampian if required providing they are well informed about this choice.

 Pre-mixed insulins - biphasic 
   [img: note]   
 Pre-mixed insulin is a combination of short- and intermediate-acting insulins and is taken before breakfast and before the evening meal. Depending on lifestyle and patient preference both people with type 1 and type 2 diabetes may choose this regimen.
 Twice daily pre-mixed analogue insulins (e.g Humalog ® Mix 25 and Novomix ® 30) have an immediate onset of action and are therefore taken immediately before food.
 Twice-daily human pre-mixed insulin e.g Humulin ® M3 has a delayed onset of duration (up to 30 minutes) and may be a safer choice than analogue preparations in patients requiring insulin administration by carers to allow enough time to prepare a meal after insulin administration.
 Patients with long duration diabetes may be on pre-mixed animal insulins. These would not be recommended for a new insulin start.

 Metformin 
   [img: note]   
 First-line option, in addition to lifestyle measures start metformin. 
 Metformin
 is the treatment of choice for people with type 2 diabetes (with no osmotic symptoms)
 can be used as monotherapy or in combination with other antidiabetic drugs
 can be continued in some people with type 2 diabetes who ultimately require insulin therapy, to maximise insulin sensitivity and minimise weight gain on insulin
 Modified-release tablets are restricted to use in patients:
 who are intolerant of standard-release metformin
 where prolonged-release allows the use of a dose not previously tolerated
 where a once-a-day preparation offers a clinically significant advantage over standard-release tablets

 Sulfonylureas (sulphonylureas) 
   [img: note]   
 Sulfonylureas:
 potentiate the pancreatic beta-cell insulin release in response to glucose
 are associated with hypoglycaemia (caution should be taken in the elderly) and weight gain
 are the primary drug treatments in people with type 2 diabetes with osmotic symptoms
 are alternative agents for overweight type 2 patients unable to tolerate usual first-line agents
 used in combination with other antidiabetic drugs 
 should be used with caution in people with mild/moderate renal impairment and avoided in those with severe renal impairment
