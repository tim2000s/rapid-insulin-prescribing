TAS Minutes / Decision:
This is a licensed, more concentrated product which could be used in place of U500 insulin. TAS
members were broadly supportive of use but concerns expressed about the number of insulins available,
particularly as biosimilar insulin glargine has just been launched. The speciality is asked to review current
choices with a view to rationalisation. Further members wished to know if this would replace U500 insulin
completely
TAS decision: Deferred

October 15:
Feedback from diabetologists was that this couldn’t directly replace an existing product but it has a
specific place in therapy. Re rationalisation of products there were some concerns within the speciality
that not having an extensive range of products available could result in missed doses with dire
consequences. Notwithstanding they would look at currently available products with a view to
rationalisation but with the caveat above. LG advised that she could supply current primary care data to
support this review. There were still governance and risk concerns expressed by the committee about the
number of products available. It was agreed by TAS that following establishing current product use if we
could identify small usage of specific products then controlled switching could be considered.

TAS decision: Supported for use. Review of current prescribing to be undertaken with a view to reducing
the number of products available

Background
In patients with type 2 diabetes requiring insulin, basal insulin is often preferred as they are associated with fewer
incidents of hypoglycaemia and less weight gain. This new 300unit/ml insulin glargine has been shown to be safe
and effective when compared to 100units/ml insulin glargine [1].

Current Use (Local)
Long acting insulins on the Leicestershire Medicines Formulary include insulin glargine, insulin detemir and insulin
degludec. In addition there are several patients managed on U500 insulin (Humulin R) [2].

Current Use (Other centres)
Not known

Clinical Trials
The key trials investigating the safety and efficacy of Toujeo® come from the EDITION series. EDITION 1, 2 and 3
were all multi-centre, randomised, open-label, two arm, parallel group, phase III studies enrolling adults with a
diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Each study included a 2-week screening phase, a 6-month treatment period
followed by a 6-month safety extension period. EDITION 4 compared insulin glargine 300 units/mL with insulin
glargine 100 units/mL in patients with type 1 diabetes.

EDITION 1
Patients enrolled in EDITION 1 were current users of basal (≥42 units daily) and mealtime insulin therapy with an
HbA1c between 7.0 and 10%. Of the 807 patients enrolled, 404 received insulin glargine 300 units/mL (Gla-300) and
403 received insulin glargine 100 units/mL (Gla-100). The primary end point was HbA1c change from baseline to
month 6. The main secondary efficacy endpoint was the percentage of patients with one or more confirmed (≤3.9
mmol/L) or severe nocturnal hypoglycaemic event reported between week 9 and month 6. Baseline HbA1c was
8.15% and 8.16% for the Gla-300 and Gla-100 groups respectively. At the end of the 6 month treatment period mean
HbA1c had decreased to 7.25% (SD 0.85) with Gla-300 vs. 7.28% (SD 0.92) with Gla-100 which was shown to meet
the non-inferiority criteria (LS mean change -0.83% (SE 0.06) for both groups, difference -0.00%; 95%CI -0.11 to
0.11). The proportion of participants with one or more confirmed hypoglycaemic event or severe nocturnal
hypoglycaemia in the Gla-300 group was 36% compared to 46% in the Gla-100 group (p=0.0045) [3].

EDITION 2
Patients enrolled in EDITION 2 were current users of basal insulin (≥42 units daily) and oral antihyperglycaemic
drugs (except sulphonylureas) with an HbA1c between 7.0 and 10%. A total of 811 patients were randomised to
receive Gla-300 (n=404) or Gla-100 (n=407). Efficacy and safety analysis included change in HbA1c, total daily
insulin dose, body weight and confirmed (≤3.9 mmol/L) hypoglycaemic events. The mean HbA1c decreased from
baseline to month 12 in both groups. At month 12 the mean HbA1c for Gla-300 was 7.62% (SD 1.03) vs. 7.64% (SD
1.21) in the Gla-100 group (LS mean difference -0.06; 95%CI: -0.22 to 0.1%; p value not stated).Over the treatment
period insulin doses increased in both groups. The mean dose of Gla-300 was 0.97 units/kg/day compared to 0.87
units/kg/day of Gla-100 (mean difference 0.11 (SE 0.02), p<0.0001 at month-12). The mean change (SD) in body
weight was statistically significantly lower with Gla-300 (0.4 (4.1) kg) than Gla-100 (1.2 (3.6) kg) (LS mean difference
at month 12 was -0.7; 95% CI: -1.3 to -0.2kg; p=0.009). The number of patients with confirmed hypoglycaemic
events was lower in the Gla-300 group compared to the Gla-100 group (numbers not stated) however this was not
statistically significant [4].

EDITION 3
Patients enrolled EDITION 3 were insulin naive patients using oral anti hyperglycaemics, with an HbA1C between 7
and 11%. Patients were randomised to receive Gla-300 (n=439) or Gla-100 (n=439). The primary endpoint was
change in HbA1C from baseline to month 6. The main secondary end point was the percentage of patients
experiencing one or more nocturnal confirmed or severe hypoglycaemic event reported between week 9 and month
6. Additional secondary endpoints included mean change in basal insulin dose, and body weight. The mean
decrease in HbA1c was equivalent in the two treatment groups at month 6 (LS mean difference in change of HbA1c:
0.04%; 95% CI -0.09 to 0.17). Therefore non-inferiority was shown. The percentage of patients experiencing at least
one nocturnal hypoglycaemic event was 16% with Gla-300 vs. 17% with Gla-100 (RR 0.89; 95% CI 0.66 to 1.2). The
mean insulin dose increased in both groups over the treatment period but more so in the Gla-300 group to mean
dose of 0.62 (0.29) units/kg/day with Gla-300 and to 0.53 (0.24) units/kg/day with Gla-100. Weight gain was lower
with Gla-300 (LS mean increase 0.49kg; 95% CI 0.14 to 0.83) compared to Gla-100 (LS mean increase 0.71kg 95%
CI 0.36 to 1.06) although this difference was found to be non-significant [5].

EDITION 4
Patients enrolled in this study had type 1 diabetes, an average HbA1c of 8.1% and used basal-bolus insulin. A total
of 549 patients were randomised 1:1:1:1 to received once daily Gla-300 or Gla-100, morning or evening, while
continuing their mealtime insulin. Gla-300 was non-inferior to Gla-100 for HbA1c change from baseline (primary
endpoint) (LS mean change [SE] - 0.40 [0.05] % and - 0.44 [0.05] %; LS mean difference 0.04 [95% CI: - 0.10 to
0.19] %). Event rates of confirmed (≤3.9 mmol/L]) or severe hypoglycemia at any time (24 h) were similar for the two
groups; nocturnal hypoglycemia was lower for Gla-300 versus Gla-100 during the first 8 weeks. Severe
hypoglycemia was observed in 6.6% (Gla-300) and 9.5% (Gla-100) of participants. Neither glycemic control nor
hypoglycemia differed between insulins or times for morning and evening injection groups. Total insulin dose
increased more with Gla-300 versus Gla-100 (change from baseline + 0.19 versus + 0.10 U/kg). However weight
gain was significantly lower with Gla-300 versus Gla-100 (LS mean difference - 0.56 [-1.09 to -0.03] kg, p = 0.037)
[7].

Adverse Effects / safety issues
Across the EDITION 1, 2 and 3 studies no between-treatment differences in safety profile were identified. Treatment-
emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were reported by 712 (57.3%) participants in the Gla-300 groups and 669
(53.7%) participants in the Gla-100 groups. The most common TEAEs were infection and infestations, nervous
system disorders, GI disorders, musculoskeletal and connective tissue disorders and administration site conditions.
In total, across all 3 studies 17 (1.4%) Gla-300 participants and 16 (1.3%) Gla-100 participants discontinued
treatment due to a TEAE. No deaths were considered related to the study mediation in any of the trials [1]. No
difference was seen between the groups in the EDITION 4 study [6]

Other points to note
    NICE are considering an insulin glargine biosimilar and are due to publish an ESNM for this in October 2015.
    The U100 and U300 glargine preparations are not bio equivalent and a dose conversion is needed. When
       switching from insulin glargine 100 units/ml to Toujeo a higher dose (approximately 10-18%) may be needed
       to achieve target ranges for plasma glucose levels [7]
    U500 is excluded to tariff; Insulin glargine 300units/mL is not.

Additional Information supplied by requestor

Place in therapy / Advantages over existing treatments. Include which drugs (if any) this will replace
Toujeo® is a long acting analogue insulin with a duration of action >24 hours after a single injection and it shows low
day-to-day variability of glucose-lowering effect within-subjects. Compared to Lantus, Toujeo® offers a more stable
and prolonged activity profile, lasting beyond 24 hours, similar rates of confirmed (≤3.9 mmol/L) total hypoglycaemia
in patients with type 1 diabetes, with similar HbA1c reduction and a lower incidence of confirmed (≤3.9 mmol/L) total
hypoglycaemia in patients with type 2 diabetes, with similar HbA1c reduction and once daily dosing with the benefit
of flexibility when needed (± 3 hours). It is a new basal higher strength insulin preparation available in the UK for both
type 1 and type 2 patients requiring analogue long acting insulin. In addition it will benefit patients who are insulin
resistant and requiring large doses of insulin. It is administered with a pre-filled pen injector that is calibrated for the
strength of insulin therefore significantly reducing the risk associated with U500 Humulin R®. (Patients on U500
currently use U100 insulin syringes which administer 5x the stated number of units on the syringe per injection.)
*Shelf life of 30 months vs. 28 days. Potential for less wastage according to dose required. Toujeo® will also be an
alternative to unlicensed U500 Humulin R® in treating insulin resistant patients who require >200 units insulin per
day to achieve adequate glycaemic control. This includes switching a proportion of those currently taking doses of
U500 Humulin R® at the lower end of the dose range, and thereafter starting insulin resistant patients on this product
before progressing to unlicensed U500 Humulin R®. Toujeo® (U300 insulin glargine) would be used to provide the
basal requirement with prandial doses of standard (U100) strength short-acting insulin. The cost of Toujeo® is
comparable to insulin glargine U100. Procurement, prescribing, and dispensing are standard rather than a unique
process requiring additional pharmacy resources to administer for an unlicensed drug.

*Not able to confirm 28 day shelf life, expiry is not reduced by out patient pharmacy and products have standard
shelf life when they are received into stock.
