 Insulin Glargine 300 units/ml  (Toujeo � SoloStar � and DoubleStar � )
 [img: View adult BNF] 
 [img: View SPC online] 
 [img: Restricted Drug]   Restricted 
 [img: Amber 2] 
 Insulins to be prescribed by brand name 
 Specialist initiation only 
 Restricted to patients already on long acting insulin analogues (e.g. insulin glargine or detemir) who:
 Experience painful injections with high volumes (>60 units in a single injection) of their current 100 units/ml long acting insulin analogue and/or 
 Suffer from recurrent episodes of nocturnal hypoglycaemia
 Toujeo SoloStar is reserved for patients on ≥60 units but <80 units daily of their current 100 units/ml long acting insulin analogue.
 Toujeo DoubleStar is reserved for patients on >80 units Toujeo 300units/ml insulin daily.
 Not bioequivalent to insulin glargine 100 units/ml dose for dose. Switches to Toujeo ® from other insulins require dose adjustment (see SPC and below for further information)

 Insulin Degludec  (Tresiba � )
 [img: View adult BNF] 
 [img: View SPC online] 
 Formulary 
 [img: Amber 2] 
 Insulins to be prescribed by brand name 
 Specialist initiation only 
 Refer to SE London IMOC recommendation for full information (link below) 
 For Type 1 diabetes in adults ; restricted to use where: 
 The diabetes is poorly controlled on both insulin detemir and insulin glargine AND 
 The next step would otherwise be an insulin pump  OR 
 Psychosocial or other factors indicate the need for longer duration insulin  OR 
 There are recurrent episodes of problematic hypoglycaemia and an insulin pump is not suitable OR 
 Some flexibility in administration time is needed where third party administration is required
 For  Type 2 diabetes in adults ; restricted to use where: 
 The diabetes is poorly controlled on both insulin detemir and insulin glargine  AND 
 Some flexibility in administration time is needed where third party administration is required OR 
 Frequent and symptomatic episodes or severe episodes of hypoglycaemia OR 
 There have been frequent emergency admissions
 Dec 2023: 
 Tresiba® (insulin degludec) FlexTouch® 100 units/ml pre-filled pens are expected to be out of stock until December 2024. 
 Do not initiate patients on Tresiba® FlexTouch® 100units/ml pre-filled pen device during this period.
 If patients are already prescribed the FlexTouch® 100unit/ml pre-filled pen device please see local guidance for alternative options.
 Dec 2025: 
 Tresiba® FlexTouch® 100units/ml prefilled pens have been discontinued

 [img: Red] 
 Specialist or hospital prescribing only. The responsibility for prescribing, monitoring, dose adjustment and review should remain with the specialist or hospital. In very exceptional circumstances, transfer of clinical responsibility including prescribing for an individual patient may be agreed between the specialist and primary care/GP.
 [img: Amber 1] 
 Suitable for initiation in primary care, following specialist recommendation. The first prescription can originate from primary care after recommendation by an appropriate specialist. The recommendation may be provided in writing, verbally, or based on clinical guidelines   
 [img: Amber 2] 
 Specialist initiation with maintenance in primary care. These medicines require specialist involvement during initiation and may require a period of treatment stabilisation before primary care prescribing is appropriate. Initial prescription(s) are issued by the specialist. If a specified minimum duration of specialist prescribing is required, this will be detailed in local recommendations   
 [img: Amber 3] 
 Specialist Initiation with shared/collaborative/transfer of care documentation. These medicines require specialist initiation/first prescription and a period of stabilisation. However, it may not be appropriate for full transfer of clinical responsibility to primary care prescribers, therefore a sharing/collaborative agreement should be in place
