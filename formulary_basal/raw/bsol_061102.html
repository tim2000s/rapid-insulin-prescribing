Insulin glargine 300 units/mL (Toujeo®)
(Ultra long-acting insulin)
Formulary

All insulins must be prescribed by brand name; preparations are not interchangeable.

High strength insulin preparation

  • 300 units per ml (high strength insulin preparation), ultra long-acting basal insulin 
  • Prescribe by brand name. 
  • Units are exclusive to the potency of Toujeo®. Due to differences in bioavailability, Toujeo® is not directly interchangeable with insulin glargine 100 units/ml or other insulins. Guidance on switching to and from Toujeo® is available here: Information on high concentration insulins. 
  • Prescribers should be experienced in initiation, titration and monitoring of insulin before using Toujeo®. 
  • Available in two devices: 
    • SoloStar® disposable pen (1 unit increments, max 80 units per single injection) 
    • DoubleStar® disposable pen (2 unit increments, max 160 units per single injection) 

 

  • Patients with type 1 diabetes where the current basal insulin is insufficiently long acting. 
  • Patients with diabetes who need help from a carer or healthcare professional to administer injections, where delays in administration of existing insulin are impacting on diabetes control. 
  • For the treatment of adult patients with type 1 or type 2 diabetes who are using ≥ 80 units per day of a basal analogue insulin (e.g. Lantus®, Semglee®) 
  • As an option for patients being switched over from discontinued Levemir® insulin. See BSol IMOC guidance for Novo Nordisk Discontinuations for more information. 

 

  • For the treatment of adult patients with type 1 or type 2 diabetes who have problematic hypoglycaemia. Oversight from a specialist is recommended until a reduction in problematic hypoglycaemia (e.g. after 3-4 months) has been demonstrated.

Insulin detemir  (Levemir®)

 Non Formulary

All Levemir® presentations are being discontinued.

  • No new patients to be started on any Levemir product
  • By 30 September 2026 the majority of switching from Levemir to an alternative should be complete, allowing time for follow-up and safe management of any outstanding patients and/or earlier than anticipated shortages
  • By 30 November 2026 all patients should be switched from Levemir to an alternative.

Joint clinical guidance on alternative options is available from the Primary Care Diabetes & Obesity Society and the Association of British Clinical Diabetologists.

NHS SPS Medicines Supply Tool outlines the key details and actions regarding the discontinuation. Note supplies of Humulin I® and Abasaglar® cannot support a significant increase in demand and should not be used as alternative.

NHSE letter to ICBs/Trusts re action needed to accelerate patient switching.

Status Description

Prescribing in children

The IMOC notes that the informed use of unlicensed medicines or of licensed medicines for unlicensed applications (‘off-label’ use) is often necessary in paediatric practice.

The IMOC advises GPs to consider specialist prescribing recommendations for Green and Amber medicines that are not subject to ESCAs or RICaDs in combination with the information provided in the BNFC which goes beyond that of marketing authorisations. The BNFC has been designed for rapid reference and the information presented has been carefully selected to aid decisions on prescribing.
  

Medicines suitable for routine use within primary care. Initiation and maintenance of prescribing by Specialists, GPs and other qualified clinicians. This may be supported by prescribing guidance, annotated within the formulary entry  

Amber Specialist Initiation: Initiation and maintenance of prescribing by Specialists and transfer to Primary Care prescribing when appropriate. This may be supported by a RICaD, annotated within the formulary entry.  

Amber Specialist Recommendation: Initiation and maintenance of prescribing in Primary Care following recommendation from a Specialist.  

Amber Shared Care: Initiation and maintenance of prescribing by Specialists and transfer to Primary Care prescribing, in accordance with an ESCA, annotated within the formulary entry.  

Medicines for initiation and maintenance prescribing by Specialists only.  

Special Commissioning Arrangements. Refer to relevant drug monograph for further information.  

Positive NICE TA and/or awaiting local clarification on place in therapy; Please contact your Medicines Optimisation team for more information.  

Non-formulary medicines, including those not yet assessed by IMOC/Trust DTC and those reviewed but not recommended for use.  

The term 'Specialist' refers to Consultants, General Practitioners with Extended Roles (GPwERs) and Independant Prescribers with a Specialist Interest.
