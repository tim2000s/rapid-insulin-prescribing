06.01.01.02    Intermediate- and long-acting insulins
Insulin Glargine
Formulary

Long-acting insulin

  • Original brand is Lantus®. Biosimilar versions are Semglee® and Abasaglar®.
  • Semglee® is currently the most cost effective biosimiliar insulin glargine, but only available in pre-filled pens. Locally 1st choice for initiations where insulin glargine in pre-filled pens is required.
  • Biosimilar insulins must be prescribed by brand name to avoid inadvertent switching. Ensure all prescriptions and communications specify brand.
  • Approximate timings
    • Onset: 30 – 60 minutes
    • Duration: 24 hours
    • Timing: Once daily at any time, preferably at the same time each day
  • Use as per Nottinghamshire APC diabetes treatment guideline and as per NICE guidance.
  • Available presentations
    • Lantus®: 10ml vial, 3ml cartridge (compatible with JuniorSTAR® (0.5 unit steps); AllStar Pro®), 3ml Solostar® prefilled disposable pen.
    • Abasaglar®: 3ml prefilled pen (Kwikpen®) & 3ml refill cartridge compatible with HumaPen Savvio®.
    • Semglee®: 3ml prefilled pens

Insulin Glargine 300 units/ml (Toujeo®)
 Restricted
  • 300 units per ml (high strength)
  • Prescribe by brand name.
  • Available in two devices:
    • Solostar® disposable pen (1 unit increments and max dose 80units)
    • Doublestar® disposable pen (2 unit increments and max dose 160units)
  • Only for use in patients that would otherwise require insulin 500units/ml
    OR  As 2nd line basal insulin after insulin glargine 100units/ml (or comparable basal insulin) in patients with poorly controlled diabetes and at least one of the following:
      - requiring twice daily basal insulin (as once daily is insufficiently long acting)
      - lipohypertrophy
      - experiencing nocturnal hypoglycaemia
      - requiring carer administration once a day, where delays in administration are impacting on diabetes control

Insulin degludec (Tresiba®)
Formulary

Tresiba®: 10mL vial; 3mL Penfill® cartridge (compatible with NovoPen® devices).

  • Note that there are two strengths of insulin degludec. 100 and 200u/ml. 
  • Tresiba® FlexTouch® 100units/mL pre-filled pens have been discontinued and are no longer available.
  • Tresiba® Penfill® cartridges should be prescribed with a compatible reusable NovoPen® device (NovoPen® 6 preferred where appropriate).
  • Do not convert (ie, recalculate) doses when transferring patients from one strength of insulin degludec to another—the Tresiba® FlexTouch® pen delivery device shows the number of units of insulin to be injected irrespective of strength.

100 u/ml cartridges

Initiation would be for only :

  • Children and young people aged 18 or under diagnosed with Type 1 or Type 2 diabetes.
  • Type 1 diabetes in adults: insulin detemir and insulin glargine remain first line options for basal insulin in accordance with NICE guidance NG17. Tresiba® can be considered as an alternative in patients where there is a particular concern about nocturnal hypoglycaemia OR for patients who need help from carer or healthcare professional to administer injections.
  • Type 2 diabetes currently treated with insulin glargine where existing basal insulin is running out OR with poor compliance OR requiring flexibility of administration OR with recurrent, particularly nocturnal, severe hypoglycaemia.

200 u/ml FlexTouch® prefilled pens.

Initiation only for patients requiring high doses of insulin that cannot be delivered using the lower strength.

Insulin Detemir (Levemir®)
Formulary

All Levemir® presentations have been discontinued (June 2025) and are being phased out. Remaining stocks are expected to be exhausted by December 2026.

  • Do not initiate new patients on Levemir®.
  • Review patients currently prescribed Levemir® and switch to an appropriate alternative basal insulin in line with local guidance.
  • Where insulin glargine U100 is selected as the replacement basal insulin, consider Semglee® as the preferred first-line local option.
  • Insulins must be prescribed by brand name to avoid inadvertent switching. Ensure all prescriptions and communications specify brand.
  • Approximate timings:
    • Onset: 30 to 60 minutes
    • Duration: up to 24 hours
    • Timing: as directed by the treating clinician, usually once or twice daily.

06.01.01.02    Biphasic insulins
Biphasic Isophane Insulin (Humulin® M3)
First Choice
  • Human biphasic isophane insulin: 30% soluble insulin, 70% isophane insulin
  • 10ml vials will be discontinued April 2026. Cartridges (for use with HumaPen Savvio reusable pen) and disposable KwikPens remain available. See Medicines Supply Notification for further information. (Reviewed July 25)
  • Insulins must be prescribed by brand name to avoid inadvertent switching.
  • Approximate timings:
    •  Onset: 30 – 60 minutes
    •  Duration: 22 hours
    • Usual timing: 15-20 minutes before meals and never at bedtime
  • 10ml vial, 3ml cartridge (compatible with Humapen®, Humapen Savvio®), 3ml prefilled Humulin® M3 Kwikpen®
 
 
Biphasic Insulin Aspart (NovoMix® 30)
Formulary
  • Biphasic analogue insulin: 30% insulin aspart, 70% insulin aspart protamine
  • As per Nottinghamshire APC diabetes treatment guideline.
  • Insulins must be prescribed by brand name to avoid inadvertent switching.
  • Approximate timings:
    • Onset: 10 – 20 minutes
    • Duration: Up to 24 hours
    • Timing: Immediately before a meal. When necessary, it can be given soon after a meal and never at bedtime.
  • 3mL cartridge compatible with Novopen®, 3mL prefilled Novomix® 30 FlexPen®.

  • Humalog Mix25 10ml vials will be discontinued April 2026. Cartridges (for use with HumaPen Savvio reusable pen) and disposible KwikPens remain available. See Medicines Supply Notification for further information. (Reviewed July 25)
  • As per Nottinghamshire APC diabetes treatment guideline.
  • Insulins must be prescribed by brand name to avoid inadvertent switching.

  • 10ml vial (Humalog Mix® 25 only), 3ml cartridge (compatible with HumaPen Savvio®), 3ml Kwikpen® prefilled pen

    		Grey / Non-Formulary: Medicines, which the Nottinghamshire APC has actively reviewed and does not recommend for use at present due to limited clinical and/or cost effective data.
    Grey / Non-Formulary (undergoing assessment): Work is ongoing and will be reviewed at a future APC meeting.
    Grey / Non-Formulary (no formal assessment): APC has not formally reviewed this medicine or indication because it had never been requested for formulary inclusion. Often used for drugs new to market.
      

    		Medicines which should normally be prescribed by specialists only. eg hospital only.
    No new patients to receive prescriptions in primary care. Patients already receiving Red Medicines in primary care should be handled on a case by case basis with the support of the Medicines Optimisation team.
      

    		Medicines that should be initiated by a specialist and prescribed by primary care prescribers only under a shared care protocol, once the patient has been stabilised.
    Prior agreement must be obtained by the specialist from the primary care provider before prescribing responsibility is transferred. The shared care protocol must have been agreed by the relevant secondary care trust Drugs and Therapeutics Committee(s) (DTC) and approved by the Nottinghamshire APC.
      

    		Medicines suitable to be prescribed in primary care / general practice after specialist* recommendation or initiation.
    A supporting prescribing guideline may be requested which must have been agreed by the relevant secondary care trust DTCs and approved by the Nottinghamshire APC.
    *Specialist is defined by the APC as a clinician who has undertaken an appropriate formal qualification or recognised training programme within the described area of practice and has a working experience and knowledge within a speciality area
      

    		Primary care/ non specialist may initiate as per APC endorsed guideline.
    The supporting prescribing guideline must have been agreed and recognised as clinically appropriate by the Nottinghamshire APC. Such guidelines will be linked to the specific medication entry of the Joint Formulary
      

    		Medicines suitable for routine use within primary care.
    Can be initiated within primary care within their licensed indication, in accordance with nationally recognised formularies, for example the BNF, BNF for Children, Medicines for Children or Palliative Care Formulary. Primary care prescribers take full responsibility for prescribing.
