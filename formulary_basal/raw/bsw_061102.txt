 Penfill® and FlexPen® will be discontinued in December 2026 
 Do not initiate new patients on any Levemir® products 
 All Levemir® products (FlexPen® and Penfill®) are being discontinued, with stock expected to run out by end of 2026. Clinicians must not initiate new patients on Levemir® and should begin identifying current users for review. 
 Switching must follow national Primary Care Diabetes Network (PCDO)/Association of British Clinical Diabetologists (ABCD) guidance (see link below), with clinical reviews by competent prescribers with careful glucose monitoring using capillary blood glucose (CBG)/continuous glucose monitoring (CGM). 
 GP practices should run searches to identify affected patients and ensure a plan for review is in place either via diabetes Multidisciplinary Team (MDT), specialists at the practice or referral to Diabetes Specialist Nurses (DSNs) as appropriate on a case-by-case basis. 
 Abasaglar is no longer recommended as an alternative U100 glargine option. 
 Lantus and Semglee remain formulary options, with insulin choice based on individual patient needs.

 December 2025 : Now discontinued -  Tresiba® FlexTouch® (Insulin degludec) 100units/ml pens: 
 Tresiba® FlexTouch® (Insulin degludec) 100units/ml pens have been discontinued. Do NOT initiate new patients on Tresiba® FlexTouch® 100units/ml pens.  See link below. 
 Tresiba Penfill® (Insulin degludec) 100units/ml solution for injection 3ml cartridges remain available and can support increased demand.  Taking into account the patient’s manual dexterity and ability to use the new device correctly. Seek advice from specialist diabetes team on use of an alternative insulin, if the above option is not considered suitable.
 Counselling points for clinicians, dispensing doctors and pharmacists. 
 Tresiba Penfill® cartridges can be used with the NovoPen 6® and NovoPen Echo Plus® devices.
 It should be noted that Tresiba® FlexTouch® U100 pens are calibrated to adjust the dose in 1-unit increments and:
 NovoPen 6® dials in 1-unit increments 
 NovoPen Echo Plus® dials in ½ unit increments .
 Ensure that patients have access to a suitable device and that the patient is thoroughly counselled on how to use this device. Further information can be found in the MSN (link below).

 HIGH STRENGTH INSULIN 300 units/mL solution for injection as Solostar® or Doublestar® pen
 high strength basal insulin
 Type 2 diabetes
 Ensure correct product is selected when prescribing. 
 SoloStar ® pre-filled pen provides a dose of 1-80 units per single injection in steps of 1 unit. 
 Doublestar ®  pre-filled pen provides a dose of 2-160 units in one injection in steps of 2 units. Doublestar is one of the very few insulin devices which administers 2 units of insulin per click, presenting a risk patients could receive double the dose if the wrong Toujeo product is dispensed or if DoubleStar® is used incorrectly by assuming one click is equivalent to one unit of insulin. 
 Criteria for use varies across BSW. Check BSW insulin guidance for further information (link below)

 Traffic Light Status Information 
 Status 
 Description 
 [img: Red] 
 RED - These medicines are considered suitable for prescribing ONLY by a specialist clinician throughout treatment. The specialist clinician is commonly situated in a hospital but may be within a virtual ward or a locally commissioned specialist-led service situated in primary care or other community setting. General non-specialist prescribing of a RED TLS medicine is NOT recommended unless there is a specific protocol under direct authority of a specialist employed within BSW set up to support this.    
 [img: Amber] 
 AMBER - These medicines are considered suitable for prescribing in primary care following specialist initiation or recommendation.    
 [img: Amber with Shared Care] 
 AMBER with Shared Care - These medicines require specialist initiation and stabilisation. Ongoing division of responsibility for drug and disease monitoring between specialist and primary care by a Shared Care Agreement (SCA). If no SCA in place status reverts to red.    
 [img: Green] 
 GREEN - These medicines are appropriate for initiation in both primary and secondary care. Prescribing is appropriate within licensed or local recommendations.    
 [img: Self] 
 SELF-CARE medicines are suitable for patients to be directed to buy themselves    
 [img: Black] 
 BLACK - Black TLS (in use from Oct 2020) Used where a decision has been made by the BSW APC not to routinely commission this preparation for its licensed indications. Do not prescribe. 
 [img: Grey] 
 Not currently used. We intend to include this TLS in future to highlight where a decision to use this medicine is under review.   
 [img: Red Specialist Centre] 
 Not currently used. We intend to include this TLS in future to highlight where this medicine and indication is ONLY available through a Specialist Centre according to a NICE Highly Specialised Technology or NHSE Specialised Commission Circular / Policy.
