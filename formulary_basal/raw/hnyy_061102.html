06.01.01.02    Intermediate- and long-acting insulins
Insulin Glargine (Abasaglar®)
Formulary
3mL cartridges
3mL pre-filled pen

  • Biosimilar insulin glargine. A cost effective brand of insulin glargine, initially for use in new patients with type 1 diabetes when clinically suitable (Note if 10mL vial required use Lantus brand). Examples of where Lantus may be more suitable than a biosimilar include during pregnancy where experience of its use is more established and also in paediatrics.

  • Type 2 diabetes - long-acting insulin analogues (LAIAs) have no advantages over human isophane insulin in effects on HbA1c levels.

    Insulin degludec (Tresiba)
    		Formulary

    Tresiba® FlexTouch® (insulin degludec) 100units/ml pens have been discontinued. Please see medicines supply notification 

     

    North Yorkshire & York

     Amber specialist recommendation 

    Available in strengths of 100 units/mL and 200 units/mL —ensure correct strength prescribed.

    100 units/mL 

    • 3mL cartridges

    200 units/mL 

    • 3mL Flextouch pen : 2– 160 units per injection, in 2 units dose adjustments

     
    Criteria : 

    • Patients with Type 1/Type 2 diabetes on a basal analogue insulin who are at risk of hypoglycaemia.
    • Patients with Type1/Type 2 diabetes who require a third party to administer their insulin.
    • Patients with mental health/memory problems who may forget to take their basal insulin at the same time each day.
    • Patients with sub optimal diabetes control who have tried other insulin’s without any improvement.

    Humber:

     100units/ml- First choice

     200units/ml- Second choice

    General Practitioner are therefore permitted to prescribe this medication provided that the suitability of initiating this product must be identified by a specialist/ diabetologist.

    Product discontinuation MSN/2025/036U (Date of issue: 16/06/2025)

    • Levemir (insulin detemir) FlexPen 100units/ml solution for injection 3ml pre filled pens and levemir (insulin detemir) Penfill 100units/ml solution for injection  3ml cartridges are being discontinued; stock is anticipated to be exhausted by the end of 2026.

    Clinicians should:

    • Not initiate new patients on any Levemir product and
    • Seek advice from specialist diabetes team when required.

    Status Description

    		Suitable for initiation, ongoing prescribing, and discontinuation in all settings. These are established therapies without the need for specialist input.  

    		Old definition - Medicines suitable for routine use within primary care and secondary care. It can be prescribed in primary care, as per the wording on the formulary and considering both the drug SPC and BNF.   

    		Old definition - Can be prescribed in primary care and by specialists in line with a recommended approved LOCAL pathway/guideline. Local = written and approved within Humber and North Yorkshire ICS. The formulary entry should link to the local pathway/guidance. (Please note - All medicines include some form of guidance, even if that is just the BNF. Green with guideline refers to the publication of approved local guidelines/pathways/protocols, etc.)   

    		Medicines usually prescribed in primary care where prior advice or recommendation from a specialist is required before initiation. Following specialist advice, prescribing and monitoring are undertaken in primary care without the need for ongoing specialist involvement.  

    		Old definition - Does not need to be initiated by a specialist but can be recommended by a specialist to primary care. No ongoing arrangements between specialists and primary care. Primary care can refer back to a specialist at any time in relation to medication query if required.   

    		Medicines that must be initiated and stabilised by a specialist. Once stabilised, prescribing and monitoring can be undertaken in primary care, where this can be safely delivered without structured ongoing specialist involvement.  

    		Old definition - Must be started by a specialist* and remain with the specialist until the patient is stable on the new medicine. It can then be transferred to primary care to continue prescribing without ongoing arrangements between the specialist and primary care. Primary care can refer back to a specialist at any time in relation to medication queries, if required.  

    Medicines initiated within specialist care and delivered across settings as part of a Shared Care Protocol

    Prescribing and monitoring may occur in primary care; however, safe use depends on:

    • coordinated care across providers
    • agreed protocols and roles
    • ongoing specialist input
    • and clear shared clinical accountability across the pathway

    These medicines are typically associated with complex monitoring, higher-risk profiles and require a Shared Care Protocol.

    		Medicines that must be initiated, prescribed, and monitored exclusively by a specialist service, regardless of setting. Primary care should not initiate or continue prescribing outside of that specialist service.  

    		Old definition - Specialist use only. The specialist initiates AND continues all prescribing and completes all ongoing monitoring.   

    		Not approved for routine prescribing in NHS-funded care. For example, because they are agents classified in the BNF as not NHS or Drugs of Low Clinical Value , or they are products on NICE s do not do list or NHS England s should not routinely prescribe list.  

    		Old definition - Not routinely commissioned. These drugs have been formally considered by the APC and are not recommended for prescribing due to, e.g. safety/cost.  

    		Not routinely prescribed unless as part of care for a long term condition. People should be supported to manage through self-care, including community pharmacy.
